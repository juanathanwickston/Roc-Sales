Module 5
Close the Sale
Objectives
By the end of this module, you will be able to:
Understand when to ask for a closing commitment.
Use trial closing questions to gauge customer readiness.
Recognize nonverbal and verbal buying signals.
Use incremental closes to guide customers to a buying decision.
Confidently ask direct closing questions with a clear call to action.
Adapt your closing approach to different behavioral styles.
Use If-Then intent questions to uncover hidden blockers.

Core Concept
A successful close is asking for a commitment to an actionable step at the right time.
There may be a series of incremental closes that lead to a buying decision.
Studies have shown that up to 2/3 of the time, salespeople do not ask for any type of commitment.
Regardless of the exact percentage, it is important to understand:
Why is there hesitancy to ask for the close?

Reflection Question
What factors might influence the willingness to ask for a closing commitment?

When Closing Becomes Natural
In most situations, closing becomes natural when you have:
Established trust and rapport.
Identified customer needs and problems.
Demonstrated a solution to those needs.
Worked through problems and concerns.
At this point, you have earned the right to ask for a closing commitment.
You will set the stage for success by practicing.

Close Action Guides
The Close Action Guides
Ask trial closing questions to get opinions and responses.
Listen to and reinforce each response.
Be responsive to buying signals.
Ask for an appropriate closing commitment.

When to Ask for the Close
The right time to ask for a closing commitment is when:
Customers acknowledge your products or services fill needs and solve problems discussed in the interview.
Concerns have been answered.
You have received positive responses to trial closing questions.

Trial Closing Questions
Before asking for a closing commitment, it is important to believe you will get a yes.
One way to identify the right time to close is by asking trial closing questions.
Trial closing questions:
Ask for an opinion, not a decision.
Help gauge readiness.
Are like feedback questions used during your presentation.

Examples:
“Do you think this will solve the problem you mentioned?”
“Are there any additional concerns we should discuss?”

In essence, you are asking:
“What concerns remain?”

Buying Signals
Another way to identify readiness is by observing buying signals.
These may be:
Nonverbal cues.
Verbal statements.

Examples of Buying Signals:
Asking clarifying questions.
Asking to see or hear more about a product.
Talking about using your product or service.
Changes in body language (leaning forward, nodding).

Reflection Question
What additional buying signals tell you the customer is ready to make a decision?

Incremental Closes
Your ultimate objective is to gain a buying commitment.
However, many sales require smaller commitments along the way.
These are called incremental closes.

Examples:
“When would it be possible for us to review the proposal together?”
“Based on our discussion, your next step is to _______. May I follow up with you tomorrow?”
“When do you have time to continue our discussion about _______?”

Activity
Using your assigned product or service:
Which incremental closing steps lead to buying decisions?

Direct Call to Action
Closing questions should be a direct call to action with clear expectations.
They should clearly define what the customer will do next.

Examples:
“Based on our discussion today, will you place an order?”
“May we begin filling out the application?”

Reflection Question
What other direct closing questions might you ask?

Closing Different Behavioral Styles
Closing effectiveness is influenced by how different behavioral styles make decisions.

Talkers
Emotional, spontaneous decisions.

Doers
Quick, confident decisions.

Controllers
Deliberate, logical decisions.

Supporters
Slow, studied, and safe decisions.

Reflection Activity
Which style do you have the greatest closing challenges with?
What specific ideas do you have for gaining commitment from this style?

Test Intent with If–Then Closes
Don’t assume the deal is real. Test it.
Use If–Then intent questions to validate commitment and uncover hidden blockers.

Examples:
“If we address the security requirements by Friday, can we get this signed next week?”
“If Legal approves the redlines, are there any other approvals we need?”
“If we hit the delivery timeline you need, is there anything else that would stop this from moving forward?”

Their answers reveal whether you’re close or just hopeful.
