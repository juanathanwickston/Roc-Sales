Identifying the Customer & Your Briefing Doc

This module helps you navigate your assigned briefing document so you can identify the customer's needs and set yourself up for success in Module 4.

1. The Sales Funnel & Your Scenario

Your handout defines exactly where your prospect is in the pipeline. Click a stage below to highlight where this status sits on your briefing sheet:

Lead (Start of Call #2 / Module 4)

Prospect (Qualified)

A qualified lead. During Call #2, you must verify their challenges and decision authority.

2. Applying the CHAMP Framework

Rather than memorizing theories, use CHAMP to parse your briefing document. Click a letter below to map that framework stage to your handout:

C — Challenges (The Customer's Pain)
Challenges are the core business problems that need solving. On your briefing sheet, check the "What You Learned in Call 1" table. These are the manual processes and leakages you will need to summarize during the call to confirm their needs.
In Module 4: Summarize these challenges during the first 60 seconds. Do not jump straight to pitching product features.
A — Authority (The Decision Maker)
This describes who has the buying power and how they make choices. Look at the "Prospect" and "Personality" lines in The Setup box. Knowing their title (e.g. Owner) and behavioral type helps you respect their position.
In Module 4: Adjust your pacing. If they are direct/skeptical, skip small talk. Validate their authority by seeking their confirmation at key transition points.
M — Money (Financial Value & Cost)
Money relates to the financial leakages and the ROI. Look at the "The Business Impact" column in the table. These concrete numbers (e.g., labor costs, EBT warnings) represent the cost of inaction.
In Module 4: You must do "The Math" out loud. Use these exact numbers to establish value before introducing any pricing.
P — Prioritization (Urgency & Time)
This shows how important this solution is compared to other tasks. Look at the "The Scenario" line in The Setup box. If the owner has only 10 minutes before their rush starts, solving this pain must save them time immediately.
In Module 4: Position your solution as an immediate time-saver, and frame the next steps around their specific busy schedule.
3. Customer-Focused Selling

The simulation measures if you understand the customer's needs before proposing solutions:

Product-Focused (Fails)

Pitching product features immediately without referencing the previous call or confirming their needs.

Customer-Focused (Succeeds)

Acknowledge their time, summarize their exact problems from Call 1, confirm they are correct, and then tie features to those needs.

Instant Fail Trigger: Opening the simulation like a cold call is an automatic fail. You must use your briefing sheet to show you remember their business situation from Call 1.
