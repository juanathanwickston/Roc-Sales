# Sales Rep Briefing: Carla Reyes (Studio Collective Salon)
## Course Integration: LMS Modules 1–3 to Simulator Handoff

This briefing document aligns the ROC Sales Process stages with the merchant profile of Carla Reyes. It serves as the foundation for the content sales reps will study in LMS Modules 1–3 before taking the live simulation in Stage 4.

---

## Stage 1 – Identifying the Customer (LMS Module 1)

### 1. The Sales Funnel & ICP Alignment
*   **Target Segment:** High-end beauty salons, wellness studios, and shared spaces with booth renters.
*   **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) Match:** Studio Collective Salon is a premium salon in Metro Detroit. It houses 6 independent stylists who rent booths and share a common reception area.
*   **Funnel Status:** 
    *   *Suspect:* Fits the profile, but has not shown interest.
    *   *Lead:* Carla downloaded a guide on "Simplifying Split-Payment Checkout in Shared Salons."
    *   *Prospect (Qualified):* The rep must verify Carla's challenges and decision authority during the call.

### 2. CHAMP Qualification Framework
Before the call, the sales rep should project how CHAMP will apply to Carla:
*   **Challenges:** Checkout confusion at the shared reception desk, disjointed client experience (some stylists accept Venmo, others use cash), manual end-of-day reconciliation.
*   **Authority:** Carla Reyes is the Owner/Operator. She rents booths to the stylists and manages the facility. She has final decision authority but cares deeply about staff consensus.
*   **Money:** Value is defined by team harmony and client experience, rather than just transaction fees. She is willing to invest in a system that makes the salon feel premium and reduces stress.
*   **Prioritization:** Solving checkout chaos is a high priority because it affects client retention and stylist satisfaction.

### 3. Product Fit: Roc Terminal+
The correct product for this merchant is **Roc Terminal+**. It addresses:
*   **Multi-Merchant Split Payments:** Allows booth renters to accept payments on a single physical terminal while routing funds directly to their separate business accounts.
*   **Unified Client Experience:** Replaces disjointed Venmo/cash workflows with a professional, centralized checkout counter.
*   **Simplified Reporting:** Tracks rentals and transactions separately for clean bookkeeping.

## Stage 2 – Ask for the Appointment (LMS Module 2)

### 1. First Impressions & Behavioral Style Adaptation
*   **Merchant Behavioral Style:** Supporter (relationship-driven, warm, collaborative, highly cautious of disruption).
*   **Rep Communication Strategy:**
    *   *Do:* Establish a warm and friendly tone, emphasize partnership, provide reassurance about staff onboarding, and focus on simplicity.
    *   *Avoid:* High-pressure sales tactics, overwhelming technical jargon, or suggesting sweeping, abrupt changes.
*   **Elevator Pitch:**
    *   *Problem:* Salons with booth renters often struggle with checkout confusion, leaving clients waiting while stylists figure out separate payments.
    *   *Solution:* We help salon owners unify their checkout counter with a system that splits payments automatically.
    *   *Unique Value:* A stress-free checkout experience that keeps booth renters independent and clients happy.

### 2. Acknowledging the Interruption & Setting the Call
*   **The Premise:** During prospecting, the rep reached out to Carla, acknowledged that managing a busy salon is demanding, and asked for a brief, low-stress call. Carla agreed to chat for a few minutes.
*   **The Handoff:** The rep must open the call with a warm, collaborative introduction, showing respect for Carla's time and her team's environment.

---

## Stage 3 – Prep for the Appointment (LMS Module 3)

### 1. Designing Need Development Questions
The rep must prepare specific questions using the **GAP Model** to guide Carla from her current state to a desired state.

| GAP Element | Rep Strategy / Question Design | Target Information to Uncover |
|---|---|---|
| **Current Situation** | *"How do your stylists currently collect payments at checkout?"* | Stylists using disjointed cash/Venmo workflows or separate personal terminals. |
| **Risks / Consequences** | *"How does having multiple payment workflows affect checkout times and client satisfaction?"* | Desk confusion, payment errors, unprofessional client impressions. |
| **Desired Situation** | *"What would a smooth checkout look like for your reception staff and stylists?"* | A single terminal that routes money to individual accounts seamlessly. |
| **Benefits / Rewards** | *"How would reducing checkout stress impact your relationship with your stylists?"* | Higher retention, happier clients, and less daily administrative reconciliation. |

---

## Stage 4 – Make the Sale (Simulator Call 1)

### 1. Customer-Focused Discovery
*   **Goal:** Focus on team harmony and customer experience. If you pitch too early, Carla will object: *"We already have a system."*
*   **Active Listening:** Match her collaborative tone. Reassure her that implementation is smooth.
*   **Uncovering Hidden Pain:** Uncover checkout confusion -> then manual reconciliation time -> and finally, the fear of losing stylists if operations feel chaotic.
*   **Next Step Close:** Ask for a follow-up call to show how the system handles split payments in a live scenario, ensuring she can invite a lead stylist if she wants.

---

## Stage 5 – Close the Sale (Simulator Call 2)

### 1. Linking Solution to Needs
*   Reference the specific concerns from Call 1 (e.g., receptionist stress, stylist payment tracking, client experience).
*   Present **Roc Terminal+** as a simple, unified checkout solution.
*   Emphasize the ease of onboarding and direct routing features.

### 2. Handling Objections and Closing
*   Anticipate her concern: *"What if my team struggles with the technology?"*
*   Provide reassurance, emphasize training support, and secure a collaborative commitment.
