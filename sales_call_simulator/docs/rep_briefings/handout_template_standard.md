# Specification: Standardized Handout & LMS UX/L&D Guide

This document establishes the standardized layout for the **Simulator Briefing Handouts** across all four persona scenarios (Sam Patel, Carla Reyes, Mike Turner, Pastor David Miller). It also outlines the L&D pedagogical strategy and UI/UX adjustments for LMS Modules 1–3 to ensure reps can scan their sheets in 0.5 seconds under "combat" pressure.

---

## Part 1: The UI/UX Handout Redesign (Combat Sheet)

Reps struggle with **cognitive overload** during active simulations. The redesign transforms the briefing document from a wall of text into a **three-stage, color-coded workflow** matching the chronology of the call.

### Why this UI/UX works for the learner:
1. **Paces the Call**: The color coding tells the rep where to look based on the timer (Blue ➔ Green ➔ Red).
2. **Eliminates Math Stress**: The calculation is pre-packaged and placed directly in the row with the pain point. They don't have to look at the top of the page for details, the middle for the pain, and the bottom for the script.
3. **Conversational Cues**: Key scripts are highlighted with distinct icons (`🗣️` for active speech, `🛡️` for defense, `🚫` for boundaries) making them pop instantly during scanning.

## Part 2: L&D Pedagogical Strategy for LMS Modules 1–3

From an Instructional Design (L&D) perspective, the LMS shouldn't just *show* the handout; it must build **cognitive mapping and active recall**.

### 1. Progressive Disclosure (Don't overwhelm)
Instead of displaying the entire document at once, the LMS walks the learner through the page step-by-step:
*   **Step 1**: Display the **Blue Card (Stage 1)**. Explain how they use the "Prospect" and "Personality" lines to adapt their tone (e.g. Controller vs. Supporter communication styles).
*   **Step 2**: Display the **Green Card (Stage 2)**. Teach them how to read the grid left-to-right to form "Features-to-Benefits" statements.
*   **Step 3**: Display the **Red Card (Stage 3)**. Explain the logic of a cushion and the structure of the 2-Option Close.

### 2. Active Retrieval Practice (The "Combat Drills")
Instead of passive reading, we add **Micro-Simulator Drills** inside the LMS. After explaining a section of the handout, we present a situation and ask the rep to click on the correct spot of the handout mockup.

#### **Drill 1: Identifying the Buyer Style**
*   *LMS Prompt*: "Sam starts speaking. He sounds rushed, skeptical, and tells you he has no time for fluff. Where on your handout tells you how to adapt to this communication style?"
*   *Learner Action*: Clicks on the **Personality** block inside the **Blue Card**.
*   *L&D Feedback*: *"Correct! Sam is a Controller. Keep your answers direct and skip the small talk."*

#### **Drill 2: Doing the Math**
*   *LMS Prompt*: "Sam objects: 'Wasted labor isn't costing me that much.' Where on your handout is the exact calculation you need to read out loud to handle this?"
*   *Learner Action*: Clicks on the **The Math & Value** column in the **Green Card** next to Aisle walks.
*   *L&D Feedback*: *"Correct! You should say: 'You mentioned paying staff for 6 hours a week at $15/hr. That is $9,360 a year in manual aisle walks. Bodega AI pays for itself.'"*

#### **Drill 3: Shielding the Objection**
*   *LMS Prompt*: "Sam says: 'I'm worried my staff won't know how to use this.' Where is your script to cushion this objection?"
*   *Learner Action*: Clicks on the **Objection Cushion** block in the **Red Card**.
*   *L&D Feedback*: *"Correct! You should say: 'That is a very common concern. What worries you most: the downtime during switching or the learning curve for the staff?'"*
